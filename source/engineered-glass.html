Engineered glass

Precision-built. Optically clear. Structurally believable.

Omnipair’s engineered glass makes financial ideas tangible. Defined surfaces, visible thickness and controlled reflections give each object the presence of a manufactured instrument. The material reveals the form and the relationship it represents.

01 · Start with meaning

Define the idea in one sentence before modelling: a deposit, exchange, amplification, access, verification or a unified system. Give each component a role. Show a clear destination for a deposit, a visible connection between connected parts, and a readable input/output relationship for amplification. Use symbols only when their meaning supports the message; do not imply guarantees or risk-free outcomes.

02 · Construct the surface

Build from deliberate discs, capsules, rounded rectangular solids, slots and cutouts. Keep front faces, sidewalls and bevels visibly distinct. Use consistent radii and thickness within each object family. Curves should feel controlled; avoid inflated faces, sagging contours and soft transitions that erase the construction. Symmetry can establish stability; asymmetry should describe function or movement.

03 · Preserve the mark

Retain the original symbol’s proportions, negative spaces and characteristic silhouette. Engrave, inset or extrude it with clean boundaries. Bevels and refraction must not distort it into another symbol. Choose the camera angle and focal plane so the primary mark remains recognizable at the final display size.

04 · Give glass substance

PropertyRequired treatment
ThicknessVisible depth with a clear separation between front face and sidewall.
FacesClean, stable surfaces. Broad reflections describe the planes without washing them out.
EdgesPrecisely bounded bevels that catch light and retain the silhouette.
TransparencyClear or dark-reading glass with enough contrast to reveal its volume.
RefractionInternal depth and edge complexity without scrambling the main symbol.
FinishPolished optical clarity. Local frosting only when it serves a specific purpose.

Avoid jelly, soft resin, melted plastic and bubble-like shells. Thickness alone is insufficient: the surface must feel rigid and deliberately cut.

05 · Use colour through light and depth

Anchor the treatment in Sky, Aqua and Dawn. Keep clear or neutral areas across the main face. Concentrate colour in edges, thicker sections and reflections. Blue/cyan and amber may share prominence; a fixed dominance ratio is not required. Local spectral highlights, including violet or green, are permitted as optical accents. Avoid an all-over rainbow coating or unrelated coloured fills.

06 · Light the construction

Use broad reflections to reveal faces and narrow highlights to define bevels and thickness. Preserve dark areas for contrast. Allow bright optical accents, but contain bloom so it does not dissolve edges. Glass should read through reflection, transmission and refraction; avoid a uniformly emissive neon outline. Add contact shadows where objects touch a surface.

07 · Add carbon steel where it works

Use blackened carbon steel for housings, grippers, frames, bases or connectors when the concept needs structure. Keep the finish dark and satin, with restrained machining and highlights that reveal its shape. Polished contact points are acceptable. Carbon steel does not mean carbon-fibre weave, and it is not required in every image.

08 · Match detail to the object

Simple tokens can remain minimal. Asset-specific engraving, fine relief and mechanical joints may be intricate when they preserve identity or explain function. Keep a clear hierarchy between silhouette, main symbol and secondary detail. Avoid arbitrary screws, ribs or seams added solely to suggest engineering.

09 · Compose for recognition

Choose a three-quarter view when it reveals thickness without losing the main face. Frontal and top-down views are valid when they explain the idea better. Avoid extreme perspective. Keep one dominant object or interaction, with purposeful supporting elements and generous space for copy. Floating and repeated tokens are allowed when they establish a clear relationship or hierarchy.

10 · Separate focus from softness

Selective depth of field may soften secondary objects or distant edges. Keep the main symbol and enough of the hero object’s construction in focus to read clearly. Optical blur can create depth; rounded, inflated geometry weakens the material even when rendered sharply.

11 · Keep the setting subordinate

Use near-black or dark charcoal as the default setting. Restrained Sky, Aqua and Dawn atmosphere and subtle dither or pixel texture are allowed. Keep texture outside critical edges and lettering. Avoid busy scenery, decorative particles and flares that compete with the object. Compose for the final crop and check the result at feed size.

What to avoid

12 · Keep a coherent series

Within a campaign, keep the bevel language, glass clarity, steel finish, colour behaviour, lighting direction and background treatment consistent. Match thickness within related object families rather than imposing one thickness on every form. Vary detail and camera framing to suit the message while preserving the same material character.

Before approval
  1. Silhouette: Does the form remain recognizable without its optical effects?
  2. Meaning: Can the visual’s idea be stated in one sentence?
  3. Material: Are the faces, thickness and bevels visibly solid and precise?
  4. Restraint: Does every supporting element earn its place?
  5. Recognition: Are the main icon and interaction readable at the intended size?
  6. Consistency: Does the render belong beside the approved references?
